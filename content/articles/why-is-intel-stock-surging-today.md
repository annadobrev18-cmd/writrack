### Earnings Report: A Key Catalyst

One of the primary reasons for Intel's stock surge is its recent earnings report. The company released its quarterly earnings, which exceeded analyst expectations. This positive earnings surprise has led to a significant increase in investor confidence, resulting in a surge in the stock price.

| Financial Metrics | Q2 2024 | Q2 2023 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Revenue | $19.4B | $15.3B |
| Net Income | $3.8B | $2.5B |
| Earnings Per Share (EPS) | $1.23 | $0.83 |

As seen in the table above, Intel's revenue and net income have increased significantly compared to the same quarter last year. The company's EPS has also seen a substantial increase, which is a key metric for investors.

### Sector Rotation: A Shift Towards Value Stocks

Another factor contributing to Intel's stock surge is the current sector rotation in the market. Investors are shifting their focus from growth stocks to value stocks, and Intel is benefiting from this trend. The company's stock is considered a value play, with a relatively low price-to-earnings (P/E) ratio compared to its peers.

#### Peer Comparison

| Company | P/E Ratio |
| --- | --- |
| Intel | 12.5 |
| AMD | 25.1 |
| NVIDIA | 30.4 |
| Texas Instruments | 20.5 |

As seen in the table above, Intel's P/E ratio is significantly lower than its peers, making it an attractive value play for investors.

### Global Ripple Effects: Impact of Trade Tensions

The ongoing trade tensions between the US and China have had a significant impact on the technology sector. However, Intel's diversified revenue stream and strong relationships with Chinese companies have helped the company navigate these challenges.

#### Impact on Supply Chain

The trade tensions have resulted in a significant shift in the global supply chain, with companies looking to diversify their manufacturing operations. Intel has been investing heavily in its manufacturing capabilities, which has helped the company reduce its dependence on any one region.
