### Historical Context: Understanding Lock-up Provisions

Lock-up provisions are agreements between companies and their shareholders, typically investors who have purchased shares in a private financing round, that restrict the sale of those shares for a specified period. This is often done to prevent a flood of shares from hitting the market at once, which could drive down the stock price. In the case of SpaceX, the lock-up provision was put in place to ensure a stable transition to public markets.

#### Previous Examples of Lock-up Provisions

To understand the significance of the lock-up provision expiration for SpaceX, it's helpful to look at previous examples. Companies like Facebook and Uber have had similar provisions in place, with mixed results. When Facebook's lock-up provision expired in 2012, the stock price plummeted, while Uber's expiration in 2019 had a more muted impact. The difference in outcomes highlights the complexity of market reactions to such events.
