### Historical Context: The Evolution of Crypto Regulations

To understand the current state of crypto, it's essential to look back at how regulations have evolved. From the early days of Bitcoin to the current diverse landscape of cryptocurrencies and blockchain technologies, the regulatory environment has been a mixed bag. Initial reactions ranged from outright skepticism to cautious engagement, with various governments and regulatory bodies adopting different approaches. The United States, in particular, has seen a fragmented regulatory landscape, with multiple agencies claiming jurisdiction over different aspects of cryptocurrency and blockchain activities.

#### Key Milestones in Crypto Regulation

- **2013:** The Financial Crimes Enforcement Network (FinCEN) issues guidelines stating that virtual currency exchanges and administrators are considered money service businesses under the Bank Secrecy Act.
- **2014:** The IRS announces that it will treat Bitcoin as property for tax purposes, rather than as currency.
- **2017:** The SEC issues a report concluding that DAO tokens are securities, marking a significant turning point in the regulation of initial coin offerings (ICOs).
- **2020:** The Office of the Comptroller of the Currency (OCC) allows national banks to provide custody services for cryptocurrencies.
- **2022:** The Lummis-Gillibrand Responsible Financial Innovation Act is introduced, aiming to provide a comprehensive regulatory framework for digital assets.

### Valuation: Assessing the Worth of Crypto Assets

Valuing crypto assets is a complex task due to their unique characteristics and the speculative nature of the market. Traditional valuation methods, such as discounted cash flow analysis, are not directly applicable. Instead, market capitalization, which is the total value of all coins in circulation, is often used as a proxy for valuation. However, this method has its limitations, as it does not account for the underlying utility or potential adoption rates of the cryptocurrency.
