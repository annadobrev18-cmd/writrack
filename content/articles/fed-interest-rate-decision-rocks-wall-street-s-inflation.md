#### The Great Recession and the Era of Quantitative Easing
The 2008 financial crisis marked a significant turning point in the Fed's approach to monetary policy. In response to the crisis, the Fed implemented quantitative easing (QE), a program aimed at injecting liquidity into the financial system by purchasing large quantities of assets, such as mortgage-backed securities and Treasury bonds. This unprecedented move helped stabilize the economy but also raised concerns about the potential for inflation.

The impact of the interest-rate decision on equity markets is multifaceted. While some sectors, such as financials and energy, may benefit from higher interest rates, others, like technology and consumer staples, may face headwinds due to increased borrowing costs.
