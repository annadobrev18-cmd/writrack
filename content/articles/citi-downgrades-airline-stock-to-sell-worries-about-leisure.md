## Fundamentals of JetBlue Airways
JetBlue Airways is a major American low-cost airline that operates on over 1,000 daily flights. The company was founded in 1998 and is headquartered in Long Island City, New York. JetBlue's business model focuses on providing high-quality customer service at affordable prices, which has enabled the airline to establish a strong presence in the US market.

### Historical Performance
In recent years, JetBlue Airways has experienced significant growth in terms of revenue and profitability. The airline's revenue has increased from $7.7 billion in 2019 to $8.3 billion in 2022, representing a compound annual growth rate (CAGR) of 3.5%. Similarly, the company's net income has grown from $569 million in 2019 to $697 million in 2022, representing a CAGR of 6.5%.

## Risk Factors
Citi's downgrade of JetBlue Airways to 'sell' is primarily driven by concerns about the airline's exposure to leisure travel. ### Fuel Price Volatility
Fuel prices are a significant component of an airline's operating costs. Changes in fuel prices can have a material impact on an airline's profitability. JetBlue Airways has a fuel hedge program in place to mitigate the risk of fuel price volatility. However, the program may not be effective in all scenarios, and the airline may still be exposed to significant fuel price risks.

### Competition
The airline industry is highly competitive, with numerous players competing for market share. JetBlue Airways faces competition from major airlines such as Delta Air Lines, American Airlines, and Southwest Airlines, as well as from low-cost carriers such as Spirit Airlines and Frontier Airlines. The competitive landscape may lead to downward pressure on fares, which could negatively impact JetBlue's revenue and profitability.
