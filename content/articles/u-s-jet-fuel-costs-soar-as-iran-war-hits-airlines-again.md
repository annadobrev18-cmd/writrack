The recent escalation of the Iran conflict has sent shockwaves through the energy market, causing jet fuel costs to soar. This surge in fuel costs has significant implications for airlines, investors, and the broader economy. ### Fundamentals of the Jet Fuel Market
Jet fuel is a refined product derived from crude oil, and its price is closely tied to the price of oil. The demand for jet fuel is primarily driven by the airline industry, which consumes millions of barrels of fuel every day. The supply of jet fuel is influenced by a range of factors, including oil production levels, refining capacity, and transportation infrastructure.

### Impact of the Iran Conflict on Fuel Costs
The Iran conflict has disrupted oil production and transportation in the region, leading to a shortage of crude oil and a subsequent increase in jet fuel prices. The closure of the Strait of Hormuz, a critical shipping lane for oil tankers, has further exacerbated the shortage, driving up fuel costs for airlines.

#### Iran Conflict: A Timeline of Events
* January 2022: Tensions between the US and Iran escalate, leading to a drone strike on an Iranian military base.
* February 2022: Iran retaliates with a missile strike on a US military base in Iraq.
* March 2022: The US imposes sanctions on Iranian oil exports, leading to a decline in global oil production.
* April 2022: The Strait of Hormuz is closed, disrupting oil shipments and driving up fuel costs.
