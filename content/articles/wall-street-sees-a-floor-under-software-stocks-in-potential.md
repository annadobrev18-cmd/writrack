## Current Market Sentiment
The software stock sector has experienced a tumultuous journey since April, marked by a choppy ascent following a steep decline over the winter. This downturn, dubbed 'Saas-mageddon' by Wall Street, was largely driven by the integration and impact of Artificial Intelligence (AI) on the software as a service (SaaS) industry. The potential deal involving Workday, a significant player in the enterprise software solutions market, is now being seen as a potential floor under software stocks, offering a glimmer of stability and growth prospects.

### Historical Context of Saas-mageddon
The term 'Saas-mageddon' was coined to describe the severe downturn experienced by SaaS companies due to overvaluation and the rapid integration of AI technologies, which led to market corrections and investor skepticism. This period saw many SaaS companies face significant challenges, including decreased valuations, layoffs, and strategic restructuring to adapt to the new AI-driven landscape. Despite these challenges, the inherent value proposition of SaaS solutions, including scalability, flexibility, and cost-effectiveness, has continued to attract investors and businesses alike.

## Market Impact of a Potential Workday Deal
A deal involving Workday could have far-reaching implications for the software stock sector. Workday is recognized for its cloud-based financial management and human capital management software, serving a wide range of organizations from medium-sized businesses to large enterprises. The speculation around a potential deal highlights the strategic importance of Workday's offerings in the current market environment. Such a deal could act as a catalyst, signaling to investors that the sector has reached a bottom and is poised for recovery.

## Technical Analysis
From a technical analysis perspective, software stocks have shown resilience in the face of adversity. The sector's ability to bounce back from the 'Saas-mageddon' lows indicates a strong underlying demand for SaaS solutions. Technical indicators such as the Relative Strength Index (RSI) and Moving Averages suggest that many software stocks are currently in a neutral to bullish territory, awaiting a catalyst to push them towards their pre-'Saas-mageddon' highs.

### Key Technical Levels
- **Support Levels:** The 50-day moving average has acted as a significant support level for many software stocks, indicating the existence of a floor in the current market.
- **Resistance Levels:** The 200-day moving average represents a key resistance level that, if broken, could signal a long-term bullish trend for the sector.

## Expert Opinions
Experts and analysts are cautiously optimistic about the prospects of software stocks, especially in light of a potential Workday deal.
