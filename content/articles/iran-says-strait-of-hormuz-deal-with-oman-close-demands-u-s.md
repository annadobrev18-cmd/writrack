The Strait of Hormuz, a vital waterway connecting the Persian Gulf to the Arabian Sea, has long been a focal point of geopolitical tensions. Recently, Iran announced that it is close to reaching a deal with Oman regarding the Strait, while also demanding that the US yield on certain conditions. This development has significant implications for global oil prices, regional stability, and the balance of power in the Middle East.

### Historical Context
The Strait of Hormuz is a critical chokepoint for international oil trade, with approximately 20% of the world's oil passing through it. In recent years, the region has experienced increased tensions, including attacks on oil tankers, drone strikes, and naval confrontations. These incidents have raised concerns about the security of oil supplies and the potential for conflict escalation.
